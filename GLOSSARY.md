# Astrology glossary: 48 terms in plain language

## Planet

### Sun

A symbolic prompt for self-expression, the direction of effort, and a sense of vitality.

- **Read it with:** Read the planet's theme with its sign's expression style, the house's life area, and any calculated aspects; connect the symbols to the person's question and experience.
- **Example:** The Sun in Taurus can open questions about steadiness, sustained effort, and sensory experience.
- **Ask yourself:** Where are you most willing to keep investing your effort?
- **Common misreading:** The Sun is not an objective measure of a person’s whole personality, career, or life purpose.

- **Ask yourself:** What kind of daily rhythm or communication helps you feel more at ease?
- **Common misreading:** A Moon placement cannot diagnose mental health or predict an emotional event.

In modern astrology, a symbol for imagination, ideals, empathy, and sometimes blurred boundaries.

## Aspect

### Conjunction (0°)

Two planets share the same ecliptic longitude within the stated orb. In symbolic reading, their themes may feel joined or concentrated.

- **Read it with:** Confirm the measured angular distance and the chart's stated orb before naming an aspect. Then read both planet themes with their signs, houses, and the person's context.
- **Example:** Mercury conjunct Jupiter can prompt a question about how communication and a desire to expand knowledge work together.
- **Ask yourself:** When do these two themes show up together in your life?
- **Common misreading:** A conjunction can bring two themes together, but it does not guarantee harmony or good luck.

### Sextile (60°)

Two planets are about 60° apart, within the chart’s stated orb. In symbolic reading, a sextile can point to a resource that may be developed through participation.

- **Read it with:** Confirm the measured angular distance and the chart's stated orb before naming an aspect. Then read both planet themes with their signs, houses, and the person's context.
- **Example:** A Venus–Mars sextile can open a question about how a wish to connect supports action and expression.
- **Ask yourself:** What action could help you make use of this possible resource?
- **Common misreading:** A sextile does not guarantee that an opportunity will appear automatically, and it cannot be inferred from sign elements alone.

### Square (90°)

Two planets are about 90° apart, within the stated orb. Symbolically, a square can frame friction between needs that may call for adjustment.

- **Read it with:** Confirm the measured angular distance and the chart's stated orb before naming an aspect. Then read both planet themes with their signs, houses, and the person's context.
- **Example:** A Mercury–Saturn square can prompt reflection on how the urge to speak and careful review affect each other.
- **Ask yourself:** How could care and preparation support your expression instead of stopping it?
- **Common misreading:** A square is not a prediction of bad events. Explain both planet themes and check the interpretation against the person’s actual situation.

### Trine (120°)

Two planets are about 120° apart, within the stated orb. Symbolically, a trine may describe an easier flow or an available resource.

- **Read it with:** Confirm the measured angular distance and the chart's stated orb before naming an aspect. Then read both planet themes with their signs, houses, and the person's context.
- **Example:** A Moon–Venus trine can prompt questions about how care needs and ways of connecting may support each other.
- **Ask yourself:** Can you recognize this ease as a resource without taking it for granted?
- **Common misreading:** A trine does not guarantee success; an easy flow can also become habitual or go unnoticed.

### Opposition (180°)

Two planets are about 180° apart, within the stated orb. Symbolically, an opposition can highlight two needs that may require coordination.

- **Read it with:** Confirm the measured angular distance and the chart's stated orb before naming an aspect. Then read both planet themes with their signs, houses, and the person's context.
- **Example:** A Venus–Mars opposition can open a discussion about balancing cooperation with personal initiative.
- **Ask yourself:** How can you respect both cooperation and your own boundaries?
- **Common misreading:** An opposition does not guarantee conflict, separation, or hostility. Describe both sides and ask the person how they experience them.

## Term

### Ascendant

The ecliptic point rising on the eastern horizon for a given birth time and place; in this course it anchors the 1st whole-sign house.

- **Read it with:** Verify the birth data first, then use the Ascendant sign to map the twelve whole-sign houses.
- **Example:** When a practice chart confirms Virgo rising, the whole sign of Virgo is the 1st house.
- **Ask yourself:** How reliable is the birth time? If it is unknown, how should the Ascendant be shown?
- **Common misreading:** The Ascendant is not the Sun sign and cannot be reliably guessed from someone’s appearance or personality.

### Tropical Zodiac

An astrological coordinate system anchored to the equinoxes, divided into twelve 30° sign sectors.

- **Read it with:** Name the coordinate system being used and keep astrological signs distinct from astronomical constellations.
- **Example:** If Astrea shows the Sun in Libra, it means its tropical zodiac longitude falls in the Libra sector.
- **Ask yourself:** Are we discussing an astrological sign coordinate or an astronomical constellation boundary?
- **Common misreading:** The shared names do not mean astrological signs and astronomical constellations use the same boundaries.

### Whole-Sign Houses

A house system in which the Ascendant sign as a whole is the 1st house, followed by signs in zodiacal order.

- **Read it with:** Confirm a reliable Ascendant before mapping house placements; Astrea uses this system for its course examples.
- **Example:** With Gemini rising, Gemini is the 1st house, Cancer the 2nd, and Leo the 3rd.
- **Ask yourself:** Do you know which house system this chart uses?
- **Common misreading:** Whole-sign houses are not the only astrological method; house boundaries differ across systems.

### Orb

The difference between the measured angular distance and the exact angle of an aspect; a smaller orb is closer to exact.

- **Read it with:** Calculate the difference from the aspect’s exact angle, then apply the chart’s stated orb convention.
- **Example:** At 92°, two planets are 2° from an exact 90° square, so the orb is 2°.
- **Ask yourself:** How many degrees separate this aspect from its exact angle?
- **Common misreading:** An orb is a measurement convention; it does not predict how intensely someone will experience an event.

### Natal Chart

A chart calculated from a person’s birth time and place, used as the data basis for natal readings.

- **Read it with:** Record input precision, tropical zodiac, and house system; a missing birth time limits which chart layers can be shown.
- **Example:** A natal Moon in Gemini in the 10th house is a birth-chart placement, not the Moon’s position today.
- **Ask yourself:** Is this placement from the natal chart or from the sky on a selected date?
- **Common misreading:** A natal chart is not a scientifically validated measure of personality or fate, and daily transits do not change its birth data.

### Transit

The sky positions at a selected time and their calculated relationships to natal positions.

- **Read it with:** Keep the snapshot time, transiting planet, natal point, and calculated aspect explicit; do not invent events.
- **Example:** A transit Mercury aspect to a practice chart’s natal Mars can be used as an observation prompt for that date.
- **Ask yourself:** Which time snapshot does this reading use, and which natal point does it connect to?
- **Common misreading:** A transit cannot establish that a specific event will happen, and it does not replace natal data.

### Natal and Transit Positions

A natal position belongs to the birth chart; a transit position belongs to the sky at a selected time. A calculation connects them.

- **Read it with:** Confirm the identity and time of each position before discussing how a transit relates to a natal theme.
- **Example:** “Natal Venus in Libra” is a birth-chart placement; “Venus in Aquarius this week” describes a transit. They are not interchangeable.
- **Ask yourself:** Which time frame does each of these two planetary positions belong to?
- **Common misreading:** Do not turn a transit into a natal placement or treat today’s sky as a permanent personality description.

### Symbolic Reading

An interpretation that organizes chart symbols into a discussion topic within a tradition, then lets personal experience confirm or revise it.

- **Read it with:** Separate calculated facts, traditional meanings, personal hypotheses, and lived experience.
- **Example:** “Venus in the 2nd house can open a question about values” is a symbolic prompt; “you will become wealthy” is a prediction.
- **Ask yourself:** Which part of this sentence is calculated fact, and which part is your interpretive hypothesis?
- **Common misreading:** A symbolic system is not a scientific forecast, medical diagnosis, or authority over someone’s decisions.

### Birth-Data Precision

The source and reliability of birth date, place, and time; precision determines which chart details can be used.

- **Read it with:** If the birth time is missing, keep confirmed stable placements but do not infer the Ascendant, houses, or house placements.
- **Example:** If only the birth date is known, the app leaves the Ascendant and houses unknown.
- **Ask yourself:** How reliable is each placement, and which conclusions must be left out?
- **Common misreading:** Approximate input must not be presented as a precise degree-level fact.
